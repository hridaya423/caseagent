## Failures to review as possible rubric problems

- **102** (Check-out works: landlord asks for a comparison quote): The landlord had already clearly asked for a comparison quote (event 009). The agent's attempt to assign a second contractor was refused by the system. It then sent the landlord an email asking them to confirm quote-only instruction and held for the landlord, so no second quote was arranged. That redundant re-confirmation falls short of both the required action and the acceptable 'confirming a second quote is being obtained', though it is defensible given the system's approval gate. The answer also lists a failed email in the Outbox and a failed note claiming the contractor was 'instructed', which muddies what actually took effect; the first contractor was correctly not told to proceed.
- **106** (Washing machine: landlord consents to the tenant replacing it with her own): The agent correctly passed on the landlord's consent, told the tenant she may take the machine with her, assigned no contractor and held only for the tenant to confirm the swap. That much fits the required action. However, it explicitly promised that the landlord will cover the removal charge and asked for the receipt, which the rubric prohibits because 'Yes this is fine' does not clearly commit to paying. That reading is defensible, since the agency's email at event 005 asked about exactly that cost, so the rubric may be strict here. The agent also did not state that the new machine is the tenant's responsibility, though it did helpfully flag the address mismatch in the call note.
- **110** (Oven repair: tenant says the visit date is wrong): The agent did not rule on which date is right, confirmed neither date, and neither cancelled the job nor recorded the visit as done. It also spotted the weekday mismatch and the inconsistent provider and tenant names. However, it never contacted the contractor to establish the date or ask them to confirm with the tenant. It sent the tenant a holding email and escalated to a person, so the required contractor-facing step did not take effect. Handing a messy record with conflicting identities to a person is defensible, but the email repeats the wrong 'Monday 30 August' label and does not apologise.
- **306** (Assignment failed and the email bounced): The agent saw that the assignment failed (event 007) and the email bounced (event 009). It reassigned an electrician under the existing approval, sent the contractor a timing request, updated the landlord and held as Waiting for Provider, so the hold follows a real new assignment. It did not tell the tenant that the first booking failed or that the event 010 message was wrong, saying only that an electrician is 'now assigned'. It also did not identify the provider, and a 'retry' of the same provider is not clearly a different working one.
- **309** (Boiler: engineer on the doorstep with an unapproved quote): The agent emailed and texted Keystone not to start, saying the £620 quote is unapproved and the £480 approval was for Harbour's diverter valve. It also told the tenant the work is not approved, sent the landlord an approval request and email flagging that the engineer is on site, held for the landlord, and caught the Flat 5/Flat 6 address mismatch. It did not phone the landlord or escalate to a human to call, which the rubric requires given the engineer is on site and the landlord's number was hard to reach. The landlord email is clearly urgent rather than a routine chaser, so the decision is defensible even though it misses the 'call as well as email' element.
