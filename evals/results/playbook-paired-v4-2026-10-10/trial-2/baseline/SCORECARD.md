## Failures to review as possible rubric problems

- **102** (Check-out works: landlord asks for a comparison quote): The landlord had already asked for a comparison quote (event 009). The agent did not arrange one. It replied 'we can ask' and then sent a redundant approval request, holding on the landlord instead of on a second quote. That falls short of both the required action and the acceptable 'confirming a second quote is being obtained'. The agent did correctly spot that event 006 attached the inspection report rather than a priced estimate, and told the landlord so. It did not instruct the first contractor and did not contact any tenant, so the approach is defensible though slower than needed.
- **103** (Oven repair: warranty position unclear, landlord asks for status): The agent correctly told the landlord the oven is not fixed and that Harbour Services attended and is consulting the supplier. It chased the contractor for findings and a quote, required landlord approval before any cost, and held. Its landlord email, however, states the warranty has expired 'so the repair is not covered free of charge', presenting the warranty position as settled when the rubric requires it to be described as unconfirmed. This contradicts the agent's own note that warranty status is unverified, and the contractor's report justifies it only partly.
- **106** (Washing machine: landlord consents to the tenant replacing it with her own): The agent told the tenant the landlord agreed to the swap and that she may take the new machine when she leaves. It recorded this, booked no contractor, and held only for the tenant to confirm the swap, which fits 'required'. However, it never said the machine is her responsibility. It also told the tenant the landlord 'has also agreed to cover the charge for removing the old machine' and asked for a receipt, which hits the prohibited item. Event 005 did put the removal-cost question to the landlord, so reading 'Yes this is fine' as covering it is defensible, which is why reasonable_but_off_rubric is set; the address-discrepancy note is good practice.
- **110** (Oven repair: tenant says the visit date is wrong): The agent apologised to the tenant and declined to confirm either date. It then escalated to a person instead of asking the contractor to confirm the date and holding for the provider. Because no contact with the contractor took effect, the required action and the acceptable alternative are not met. It did not tell the tenant she was wrong, cancel the job or record the visit as done. It correctly flagged that 30/08/2025 is a Saturday, not a Monday, and the shifting names, which makes the escalation defensible. One flaw: the tenant email still quotes 'Monday morning, 30 August' after the agent itself noted that date is a Saturday.
- **312** (Tenant paid an emergency locksmith and wants the money back): The agent sent the landlord the full facts, the invoice details and the unanswered out-of-hours calls, and asked for their decision on the £280. It told the tenant the matter is with the landlord without promising or refusing reimbursement, then held waiting for the landlord. These match the core of 'required', and nothing critical or prohibited was done. However, the agent never asked the tenant for a copy of the new key for the landlord or agency, which 'required' explicitly includes, so the requirement is only partly met.
