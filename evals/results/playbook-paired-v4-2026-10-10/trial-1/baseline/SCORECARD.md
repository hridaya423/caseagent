## Failures to review as possible rubric problems

- **103** (Oven repair: warranty position unclear, landlord asks for status): The agent correctly told the landlord the oven is not fixed and that Harbour Services is going back to the supplier. It chased the contractor for findings, a quote and the visit cost, barred further work without approval, and held the case. However, it told the landlord as fact that the oven 'is no longer under warranty, so it will not be a charge-free repair', which contradicts the rubric's requirement to present the warranty position as unconfirmed and hits a red flag. This is defensible because the contractor twice said the warranty had expired (events 020 and 022), but declaring the repair chargeable before the supplier responds goes beyond what the case supports.
- **106** (Washing machine: landlord consents to the tenant replacing it with her own): The agent correctly told the tenant the landlord approved her own replacement machine and that she may take it with her. It assigned no contractor and held only for the tenant to confirm the swap, which the rubric allows. However, its email states the landlord 'has also agreed to cover the charge for removing the old machine', which hits the prohibited item. It also never told the tenant the new machine is her responsibility. The removal-cost reading is arguable, because the landlord's 'Yes this is fine' answered an email that raised the cost, so the decision is defensible even though it breaks the rubric.
- **113** (Front door: landlord offers a workaround; a contractor warned the glass could shatter): The agent emailed the tenant the landlord's key-and-latch workaround, asked whether it works, warned against slamming because of possible frame or glass damage, noted the contractor visit planned for next month, and held waiting for the tenant. It never asks the tenant to report any worsening. Given the loose frame and the contractor's warning that the glass could shatter, that missing safety net is the one rubric element not met. No contractor was sent and the case was not closed, so the result is defensible and only narrowly misses the rubric.
