## Failures to review as possible rubric problems

- **106** (Washing machine: landlord consents to the tenant replacing it with her own): The agent told the tenant the landlord agreed to the swap and that she may take the machine when she leaves. It assigned no contractor, recorded a note and held only for her to confirm the swap was done, which meets the core requirement. However, it also promised her that the landlord would cover the removal charge and asked her for a receipt. The rubric prohibits this because the landlord's 'Yes this is fine' does not explicitly say so, though it can be argued he was answering event 005, which asked that question directly. Flagging the address mismatch in the call note was good practice, but the cost promise should have been confirmed with the landlord first.
- **110** (Oven repair: tenant says the visit date is wrong): The agent did not rule on which date is right and did not confirm either date. It wrote to the tenant without telling her she was wrong, and it correctly noticed that 30/08/2025 is a Saturday while the slot is described as "Monday", and that names vary across events. However, it never contacted Keystone Compliance to establish the date or to ask them to confirm with the tenant. Instead it escalated to a person, so the required contractor step was not taken and the acceptable alternative (apologise and ask the contractor to confirm) was not done either; this is still a defensible handover given the heavy identity and contact confusion.
- **307** (Everything is already done): The agent correctly concluded nothing was outstanding and tried to close, but the close_case call was refused by a workflow check. It then emailed Keystone Works to resend the unavailable completion photos and put the case on hold waiting for the provider, so the required close did not happen and the hold is a red flag. Asking for missing photos is a request for evidence, not re-instructing work. Given that both completion emails showed the photos as unavailable and a policy gate blocked closure, a careful agent could defend the request. The agent also correctly noted the duplicate email and the Flat 7 boiler advert as needing no action.
- **309** (Boiler: engineer on the doorstep with an unapproved quote): The agent stopped Keystone by SMS and email, said clearly that the £620 quote was unapproved and that the earlier £480 approval was for another provider, answered the tenant, and held for the landlord. It also caught the Flat 5 / Flat 6 address mismatch. However, its only landlord contact was a single written approval request: no call was attempted and nothing was escalated for a human to phone, although the engineer was on site and the landlord's number is known to be unreliable. That misses the rubric's 'call as well as email' requirement, though the overall handling is defensible.
